Abstract

Deploying vision-and-language navigation (VLN) agents in unseen environments remains challenging because unfamiliar layouts and visual conditions can cause autonomous execution to go off track. Rather than relying on continuous human supervision, a practical strategy is to let the agent selectively request corrective guidance when needed, use it to recover the ongoing task, and reuse the resulting corrective interactions to improve subsequent navigation.

We propose Abstention-aware Visual Error Recovery and Training for Vision-and-Language Navigation (AVERT-VLN), a closed-loop framework that uses a plug-in vision-language Monitor for online human-assisted recovery and offline preference learning. The Monitor operates separately from navigation decision generation and assesses instruction–execution consistency from the instruction, visual history, and current observation. To train the Monitor for deviation recognition, we construct LostNav Dataset with 20K counterfactual risk trajectories and rule-based deviation labels. The Monitor is first fine-tuned on 40K normal trajectories to assess instruction progress and then jointly fine-tuned on normal and risk trajectories to recognize semantic deviations.

At runtime, Asynchronous Sidecar Monitoring evaluates execution alongside the navigation model. When the controller accepts a Lost verdict, it suspends autonomous execution and requests human guidance for recovery. For offline policy improvement, Trajectory-Anchored Preference Learning converts deviation-associated failures into decision-level preference pairs under shared decision contexts, restricting supervision to the decisions targeted for correction. Under human-assisted evaluation, the full AVERT-VLN system achieves success rates of 76.2% and 66.3% on the val-unseen splits of R2R-CE and RxR-CE, respectively. The same monitoring and human-assisted recovery interface also improves success rates across the three evaluated navigation architectures.

How it works

From recognizing errors to learning better decisions.

A shared vision-language Monitor connects a runtime recovery loop with an offline learning loop, without generating the navigation actions itself.

Loop 01 · Online recovery

Asynchronous Sidecar Monitoring

The Monitor reads the instruction, visual history, and current observation alongside ongoing navigation. An accepted Lost verdict cancels pending autonomous actions and requests a pixel goal or a local turn from a human. Navigation resumes after correction.

Monitor→Abstain→Guide→Resume

Loop 02 · Offline improvement

Trajectory-Anchored Preference Learning

Trace a deviation back to the nearest preceding Pass boundary. Under the same decision context, pair the original decision with a correction-derived preferred decision. Decision-focused DPO targets corrected direction-selection and visual-grounding tokens.

The shared Monitor

Learn what going off track looks like.

Small model.
Navigation-specific judgment.

A Qwen3.5-4B Monitor is trained to assess instruction progress and recognize semantic deviations—not simply whether the agent has moved away from a geometric path.

On the LostAware Benchmark, it reaches 63.38% Lost recall and 59.52% F1. The benchmark contains 1,095 paired Lost/Pass states, 2,190 states in total, held out for evaluation.

The strongest displayed baseline F1 is 49.79%. Diagnostic reasoning scores in the figure are model-judged, not measured human recovery outcomes. Higher recall does not imply uniformly higher precision.

Inside LostNav: 40K normal trajectories + 20K counterfactual risk trajectories

Two-stage Monitor training

Progress first.
Deviations next.

The Monitor first learns instruction progress from 40K normal trajectories. Joint training then adds 20K counterfactual risk trajectories from LostNav, with rule-based deviation labels and generated diagnostic explanations.

Risk generation varies explore/exploit behavior and region/object targets. Visibility and reachability checks support controlled wrong-target rollouts rather than arbitrary image corruption.

Privileged scene and navigation information supports dataset construction only. Monitor inputs remain the instruction, visual history, and current observation. LostAware is a separate evaluation benchmark, not the training set.
